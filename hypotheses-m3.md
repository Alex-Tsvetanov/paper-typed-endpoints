## 1. The generator and the server

oha 1.16.0, its release binary pinned by sha256 (`bench/pins.md`, Lab request 5): a load generator
written in Rust whose HTTP/2 client (hyper's h2) shares no code with h2load (nghttp2) or ghz
(grpc-go). Every window runs it as

```
taskset -c 2-13 oha --http2 -c 12 -p 128 -z 10s --no-tui --output-format json --worker-threads 12 \
  -m POST -D <body> -H 'content-type: application/grpc' -H 'te: trailers' \
  http://127.0.0.1:<port>/bench.e2e.Echo/<method>
```

that is, 12 connections of 128 streams each, the c12 m128 load of `hypotheses.md` section 3, on the
generator's CPUs 2 to 13, with the framed bodies h2load posts (`small.bin`, `medium.bin`). oha does
not read gRPC trailers; the server's log, which counts every call by its grpc-status, checks that
every call succeeded. The server, its build and its placement are the replication's on Linux
(request 29; one worker on CPU 14, its sibling 15 idle, boost off, after the quiet check).

## 2. The gate: does oha saturate the server? (request 32)

A pilot, run once before the campaign: for each payload (medium, then small) and arm (R, P, Q, S),
one server run of a 10 s warm-up window and 2 windows of 10 s. The gate passes if every pilot run is
valid (section 4's rules) and, for every arm and payload, the server's mean `mid` share over the 2
windows is at least 95% of one CPU, the threshold of `hypotheses.md` decision (e). The pilot decides
only whether the campaign runs. Its shares are reported as the gate's evidence, never as a contrast
or a rate.

If the gate fails, the campaign does not run, and the paper reports that this second generator did
not saturate the server, with the pilot's shares; Grok's M3 is then answered as not doable with oha.

## 3. The campaign (request 33, only if the gate passed)

Ten replicates. In each, the two payloads in a seeded order and, per payload, the four arms in a
seeded order (`bench/campaign-m3/oha-order-linux.tsv`, label "P5 oha e2e linux", seed 3397203845).
Per arm and payload, one server run: a 10 s warm-up window, discarded, then 4 windows of 10 s.

Measure. Primary: requests per second, the window's count of HTTP 200 responses over the seconds oha
ran (its `summary.total`, which on HTTP/2 includes the calls in flight at the deadline); a
replicate's value is the mean of its 4 windows. Secondary, descriptive: the server's and oha's CPU
shares per window (`mid`), and each arm's oha rate over its h2load rate at c12 m128 in the
replication.

Hypothesis. Y1: at c12 m128 under oha, R serves more requests per second than P, and than Q, for
both payloads (4 contrasts). The direction is the first campaign's, where R served more than P and
than Q at c12 m128 for both payloads under h2load, with every interval above 1. S runs as the
reference and is never compared.

Test. The exact one-sided paired sign test over the 10 replicates (a tie counts against). The 4
contrasts form their own family, m = 4, Holm at alpha 0.05 family-wise; 2^-10 = 0.00098 < 0.0125.
Effect sizes: the ratio of mean requests per second with a 95% BCa interval from a clustered
bootstrap that resamples replicates with all their arms (10,000 resamples, seed recorded).

## 4. Validity

Per server run: every window's file is clean (oha's `errorDistribution` empty, its
`statusCodeDistribution` only 200), oha exits with status 0, the server log ends with `failed 0`,
and its count of OK calls lies between the sum S of oha's counts over the run's windows (warm-up
included) and S plus 2 c m per window. In the campaign, a void run runs again once after the last
row; a second failure makes that payload's contrasts descriptive, and is reported.
